Why the last one worked when four didn’t

Every earlier attempt upgraded the judge — a better evaluation function, more fixes, more knowledge — while the search looked only two to four exchanges into the future and guessed the rest. Foul Play spends the same 100 ms differently: a best-first tree search that pours its budget into promising lines (following them 15–20+ turns deep), evaluates roughly a hundred thousand positions per decision inside a native engine, and plays the move that survived the most scrutiny rather than the one with the highest single estimate.

v4 calls that exact search — an entry point of the same open-source engine both bots already embed — fed by our own opponent-inference. First outing: 65 wins in 300 clean games, zero errors, roughly double every number the campaign had ever produced.

Honest print: the confirmed +8.0 is a paired, preregistered, artifact-gated result at equal budget — the first of its kind here. The earlier 21.7% is a single-arm observational run from a reviewed branch build, and its decisions used ~119 ms against Foul Play’s 100 ms — a compute edge the preregistered paired confirmatory (600 per arm, budget-controlled, two-sided test) exists to control. That run is the number that gets to count. All prior results carry the same statistical discipline, including the withdrawal of seven harness-artifact wins discovered by audit.

What’s next

Four parallel builds are in flight on top of the winner: our measurably better value model as the MCTS leaf, engine-fidelity fixes (the ability census), a structural fix for search-starvation on gimmick options, and full-team belief determinization — the opponent-model edge Foul Play doesn’t have. One combined measurement follows when all four land. The current brain is also live on the private Showdown server at 250 ms for real players to test — the first human rematch since the 1W–15L era, now with a fundamentally different search.
